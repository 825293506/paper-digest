In this paper, we propose a three-layer cascaded metasurface that functions as a reconfigurable beam splitter operating at 11 GHz. Through the joint rotation of the middle and bottom layers, the metasurface can split incident linearly polarized waves into circularly polarized transmitted and reflected waves with arbitrary spin combinations. This unique capability is attributed to its strip-slot-strip cascaded structure, which effectively enhances the stability of the interlayer coupling effect. Notably, both the simulated and measured axial ratios of the reflected and transmitted waves across all interlayer rotation states are found to be lower than 3 dB. Furthermore, benefiting from the electric dipole resonances excited on the top and bottom layers under oblique incidence, the metasurface maintains robust performance under 15° transverse-electric (TE) and transverse-magnetic (TM) oblique incidences. The proposed reconfigurable circularly polarized beam splitter exhibits considerable potential for applications in modern optics and satellite communication systems.

Yet both liquid-crystal and silicon OPAs suffer from notable nonlinear effects, and cascading OPAs for two-dimensional scanning further introduces alignment errors and crosstalk that are difficult to measure.
