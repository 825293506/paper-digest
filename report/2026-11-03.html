Abstract Holography, a wavefront reconstruction technique, provides a powerful approach for engineering complex optical fields. However, conventional phase-only holographic approaches for optical vortex generation remain largely confined to specific vortex modes or single-diffraction-order encoding, thereby significantly limiting their design flexibility. Here, we demonstrate an inverse-designed holographic framework based on angular spectrum propagation that moves beyond conventional paradigms. By exploiting the full spatial degrees of freedom (DOFs) of the hologram for full-field wavefront shaping, we generate diverse exotic optical vortices with well-defined intensity and phase distributions. The framework is further extended to meta-holography by encoding the optimized phase profile onto a geometric-phase metasurface, enabling the high-fidelity generation of Laguerre–Gaussian (LG) beams. This work paves the way for practical holographic complex-amplitude control and opens new opportunities in optical communications, optical tweezers, and spatiotemporal vortex generation.

Wave-based analog computing has re-emerged as a compelling alternative to digital electronics for specific linear-algebraic and machine-learning workloads, promising massively parallel, high-bandwidth, and energy-efficient information processing. Metamaterials, metasurfaces, and integrated metastructures (collectively, metadevices) now perform mathematical operations, solve integral and differential equations, multiply matrices, and implement trainable photonic neural networks directly in the wave domain. This focused review consolidates the mathematical foundations that underpin such devices, i.e., the mapping between linear operators and wave scattering, the feedback architectures that realize equation inversion, and the inverse-design and scientific machine-learning toolchains that make their synthesis and training tractable, and connects them to the rapidly growing body of experimental demonstrations, from free-space diffractive processors to reconfigurable guided-wave metastructures. We conclude with open problems and opportunities for this emerging neuromorphic nanophotonics ecosystem.
