ABSTRACT Beam steering devices are essential components in several applications, such as detection, LiDAR, and optical communication. However, traditional beam steering devices tend to be bulky or exhibit a limited steering angle. Cascaded metasurfaces exhibit strong capabilities for powerful dynamic light modulation. In this work, we propose and demonstrate a highly integrated beam steering device utilizing two cascaded, independently rotating metasurfaces. Through simple relative rotation, the output beam can be continuously and dynamically steered, generating pre‐designed trajectories that densely cover a wide angular space. We have numerically simulated and experimentally verified continuous beam steering of petal‐shaped scanning trajectory with discrete scanning technique. Furthermore, by integrating traditional gradient phase design with the Dammann grating profiles, we achieved a wider scanning range and a greater number of addressable beam positions. This technology is compact, and versatile to utilize in various fields, including but not limited to optical sensing, light detection and ranging (LiDAR), and free‐space optical communication.

查看英文摘要原文

Compact and lightweight lenses are essential for miniaturized varifocal imaging. We designed and fabricated an Alvarez metalens composed of two cascaded, phase-complementary metalenses. Continuous focal-length tuning is achieved by the relative in-plane lateral displacement of the two metalenses, which continuously modulates the synthesized quadratic focusing phase. To suppress aberrations, Zernike basis functions were incorporated into the phase profile of the Alvarez metalens. The focusing and imaging performance of the device was then experimentally validated at the design wavelength. With a 1 mm in-plane displacement, the Alvarez metalenses (4 mm aperture, 0.62 g weight, λ=532 nm) enabled focal length modulation from 10 mm to 150 mm, all while achieving an average diffraction efficiency of approximately 40%. The integration of an in-plane-actuation-only varifocal mechanism with ultrathin metalenses enables a highly scalable approach to miniaturizing systems for imaging, optical communications, and biomedical applications.
